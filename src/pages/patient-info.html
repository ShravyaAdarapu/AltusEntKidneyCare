Two specialities, connected care

When ENT and kidney concerns overlap

Autoimmune & vasculitic conditions

Joint assessment can be helpful when sinus or airway symptoms occur alongside kidney findings. Conditions such as granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA) and IgA vasculitis may require coordinated investigations and specialist follow-up.

ENT infections during kidney treatment

People receiving dialysis or transplant medicines may need coordinated review for ENT infections, including suspected fungal sinusitis or mucormycosis. The doctors can assess the next step and arrange hospital assessment when the condition requires it.

Medicines & hearing monitoring (ototoxicity)

Audiometric review may be recommended when kidney patients receive medicines that can affect hearing, including certain antibiotics or high-dose loop diuretics. Bring your full medicine list so monitoring can be planned appropriately.

Snoring, sleep apnoea & blood pressure

ENT upper-airway assessment and sleep-apnoea care can be coordinated with blood-pressure and kidney reviews. Discuss snoring, witnessed pauses in breathing and daytime sleepiness during your consultation.

Tests & treatment support

Plan the next step in your care

Your doctor recommends the tests or support that fit your needs. The front desk can help you check availability, preparation and scheduling before you visit.

Laboratory & diagnostic assessment

Kidney function tests (KFT), electrolytes, HbA1c, complete blood count (CBC), urine routine and microscopy, urine chemistry and spot protein-to-creatinine ratio. ABPM fitting/report review and point-of-care blood gas or electrolyte assessment may be considered when clinically indicated.

Audiology, hearing aids & speech

Hearing assessment, hearing-aid trials and fitting, speech therapy and voice evaluation can form part of an individual ENT care plan. Ask about appointments and any previous audiograms or reports to bring.

Pharmacy & medicine support

Review and prescription guidance for kidney medicines such as erythropoietin, phosphate binders and transplant immunosuppressants, along with ENT drops or antibiotics where prescribed. Check medicine availability with the front desk.

Day-care & minor procedures

Care planning for prescribed IV iron or IV antibiotics, minor ENT procedures, wound care and short-stay monitoring. Your doctor will discuss the appropriate facility, supervision and arrangements before treatment.

Preventive health reviews

Kidney screening, blood-pressure and diabetes reviews, hearing checks and prevention advice can be selected around your age, symptoms, family history and previous results. A package or test list is agreed after reviewing your needs.

FAQs

Common questions

Can't find your answer? Call or WhatsApp us on 99668 81811.

Do I need a referral to see the nephrologist or ENT surgeon?

No. You can book directly. If another doctor has referred you, please bring their note and your reports.

Can I consult both doctors on the same day?

Yes. Mention it when booking and we'll schedule back-to-back slots where OPD days overlap.

Is dialysis done at the clinic?

Altus consultations cover dialysis planning, coordination and follow-up. Dialysis and transplant procedures require an appropriate treatment facility; discuss the arrangements with Dr. Chetan and the front desk.

Where are ENT surgeries performed?

The setting depends on the procedure and your assessment. Minor ENT care may be planned around your consultation; operations such as FESS, tympanoplasty or tonsillectomy require an appropriate hospital setting. The front desk will explain the arrangements.

Should I fast before a kidney check-up?

Preparation depends on the tests ordered. Check your instructions with the clinic or laboratory before attending, and do not stop medicines unless your doctor advises it.

Do you see children?

Yes. Dr. Mounika sees children for ear, nose and throat problems, and Dr. Chetan evaluates kidney problems in children.

Do you offer online consultations?

Video follow-ups are available for existing patients reviewing reports. First consultations are best in person.
